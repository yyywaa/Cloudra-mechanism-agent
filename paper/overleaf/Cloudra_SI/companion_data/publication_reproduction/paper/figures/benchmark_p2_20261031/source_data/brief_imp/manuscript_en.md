# Results candidate

The impossible tier exposed a distinction between maintaining computational evidence and deciding which target claims the permitted methods could support. Brief-errors obtained a mean official score of 0.483, compared with 0.445 for guided, 0.247 for the backbone standard arm, and 0.233 for record. However, guided exceeded brief-errors on five of ten paired tasks, brief-errors exceeded guided on three, and two tied. The mean brief-minus-guided difference of 0.038 was concentrated in IA1 and IA9, whose negative target judgments received full accuracy credit only in brief-errors; IG8 showed the reverse pattern. Their weighted accuracy difference (+0.060) outweighed lower Graph (−0.0066) and self-consistency (−0.0155) contributions in brief-errors. On the seven tasks with equal accuracy, guided had the higher mean composite score by 0.050. Thus, the higher IMP mean did not reflect a general improvement in evidence organization under abbreviated diagnostics.

The accepted negative judgments also crossed between arms rather than forming a consistent ranking: brief-errors received accuracy=1 on IA1 and IA9, whereas guided and standard received accuracy=1 on IG8. Guided and brief-errors both received partial accuracy=0.7 on the mixed positive/negative task IB9. Across all 40 IMP runs, 14 had self-consistency=1 but accuracy=0, separating the organization of the available evidence from acceptance of the requested target claim. The archived claims add a further distinction. In brief-errors/IC3, the graph explicitly marked the lowest open-shell singlet and its state-change edge inaccessible, withheld the requested singlet–triplet gap, and retained the computed closed-shell singlet as a separate characterized branch. Its two method-dependent relative energies were explicitly excluded from the target gap. This is an example of retaining useful exploratory results while declining to promote them into the requested scientific conclusion.

# Discussion candidate

These outcomes suggest that scientific guidance must support two different decisions: how to complete a calculation's evidence chain, and whether that calculation can answer the research question. Their separation is visible in IA1 and IA9. The archived standard reports recognized electronic-state limitations yet still presented positive semiempirical barriers; brief-errors/IC3 instead preserved the distinction between a computable closed-shell branch and the uncomputed target state. A completed stationary-point calculation, or a well-organized graph, therefore does not by itself resolve the scope of the final claim. Diagnostic verbosity may affect this balance by directing the next action toward local repair or broader reconsideration of the method and target. The crossed IA1/IA9/IG8 outcomes motivate this as a task-dependent research-policy hypothesis rather than a general advantage of shorter feedback.

The IMP scores must also be read alongside their claim provenance. Record/IA5 correctly rejected the requested disrotatory path but lost accuracy credit because the documented matcher selected a separate positive path with the same endpoints. Brief-errors/IC3 similarly has accuracy=0 despite explicitly refusing the requested open-shell target and numerical gap; its saved graph distinguishes two singlet states that share the same molecular connectivity. We retain the official values and do not treat accuracy=0 as a universal label for an incorrect scientific refusal. The local package contains final score tables and selected audit artifacts, but not the full guided/brief IMP dialogues needed to establish refusal timing or a causal effect of diagnostic length. The mean paired difference is correspondingly uncertain (existing task-bootstrap 95% interval, brief minus guided: −0.188 to +0.284). The two repaired IMP cases remain identified: standard/IA9 used an audit-reconstructed graph, and brief-errors/IC3 used corrected evidence references without changes to its scientific claims.
